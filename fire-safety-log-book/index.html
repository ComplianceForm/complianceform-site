What's in the workbook

  • Dashboard. Every routine check with the date it was last done, when it is next due and a red, amber or green status.
  • Fire alarm log. Weekly call point tests, with drop-downs for the result and space for faults and actions.
  • Emergency lighting log. Monthly function tests and the annual full duration test, tracked separately.
  • Fire door register and checks. List every door once. Each inspection uses the five points from the Home Office fire door fact sheet and marks the door Pass or Fail.
  • Extinguisher register. Annual service dates per unit, plus a monthly visual check log.
  • Drills, training and defects. Drill times, staff refresher dates and a defects list that closes itself when you add the date fixed.
  • Servicing and certificates. Contractor visits, fire risk assessment reviews and certificate references, with next due dates.

Who it's for

Landlords and managing agents looking after blocks of flats and HMOs, office and shop managers, small care settings, churches and community buildings. In short, anyone who has been told they are the responsible person and needs a sensible way to keep the records straight.

If your building is a multi-occupied residential building in England over 11 metres tall, the fire door register is set up for the quarterly communal door checks and annual flat entrance door checks required by regulation 10 of the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022.

The workbook is a record-keeping tool. It does not replace your fire risk assessment or advice from a competent person.

Questions

Do I need Microsoft Excel?

No. It works in Excel 2019 or later, Microsoft 365 and Google Sheets (upload it to Google Drive and open it with Sheets). It does not work properly in Apple Numbers.

Is this a fire risk assessment?

No. It is a record of the routine checks your fire risk assessment and the relevant standards expect. You still need a suitable fire risk assessment carried out by a competent person.

Can I change the check intervals?

Yes. The defaults are common intervals, and every one can be changed. If your fire risk assessment, a manufacturer or a contractor gives a different interval, use theirs.

Does it cover Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?

The record sheets work anywhere in the UK, but the fire door intervals quoted come from regulations that apply in England. Check the rules for your nation.

How many buildings can I use it for?

One copy per building works best. Save a separate copy for each premises you manage. The licence covers your own buildings and the buildings you manage for clients.
